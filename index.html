Timeline

Marlin V2 is a gas-generator-cycle turbopump for a 1,500 lbf engine. A full assembly hot fire is planned for this school year; it has not been hot fired yet.

  • Help size the turbine to the pump, gas generator, and shaft based on turbine power extraction.
  • Built 2-D cascade meshes of the impulse turbine blisk, and I run OpenFOAM CFD on them to estimate blade loss and isentropic efficiency. I am responsible for finding the axial thrust on the turbine and making sure the rotordynamics can handle it.
  • Working with the turbine lead on a Python tool that turns rotor blade geometry into a CFD-ready mesh, so the results feed back into the design instead of being a one-off picture.

Started in tenth grade as the first piece of a liquid engine. Diesel and GOX was a trade: it forced me to solve atomization, kept cost down, and avoided cryogenics on a residential lot.

  • Built the test site first: about 40 tons of dirt and rock moved by hand over about 250 hours, and a stand with a blast plate behind it.
  • Designed four unlike-triplet injector plates (two oxidizer orifices, one fuel orifice) in CAD. I made them from laser-cut blanks, drilled the orifice pattern on a used drill press through a 3D-printed angular drill guide, and fixture-machined them. I taught myself TIG welding, about 80 hours, and welded all the main parts myself.
  • Used design for manufacturing (DFM) to keep cost low. The methods in the reports I studied were not available to me, so I replaced them with ones my shop could actually do. I made engineering drawings for myself to fabricate a flame suppression system.
  • Component-level testing before any hot fire: I leak tested and proof tested the hardware, then cold flowed the injectors to check the spray. I developed the injector by drilling, cold flowing, and drilling again until the fourth plate atomized well enough that I trusted it.
  • Built the test equipment for remote operation: an Arduino fire-control sequence (arm, fire, purge, and abort), a key-fob remote, and two actuated ball valves kept me off the pad. I used a pipe combustion chamber to maximize safety and combustion predictability.
  • Planned each test around safety first and ran it myself. I wrote test procedures and checklists and used them to brief the firefighter on site. Two system-level hot fires at a 1:1 mixture ratio gave 34 seconds of test data.
  • Volunteer (2023 – 2026): gave public technical talks and ran hands-on exhibit floors for thousands of visitors.
  • Intern (summer 2026): demonstrated real aerospace test hardware and helped with the museum redesign, looking at cost, accessibility, and how well it helps people learn.
  • Started the school's first aerospace club from zero. I found a sponsor and set up the charter, meetings, and build work.
